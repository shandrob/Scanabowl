In a few months a puppy grows from a handful to – sometimes – dozens of kilos. That asks a lot of the food: more energy per kilo, more protein and the right amount of minerals. But not every puppy is the same. A Chihuahua is fully grown after about a year, a Great Dane only after one and a half to two years. And with those large breeds, details matter.

## Why puppy food?

Food for adult dogs is made for *maintenance*. A growing puppy needs more. The European FEDIAF guidelines therefore have separate standards for growth, with higher minimums for protein, fat and calcium, among others. Check the pack to see whether the food is meant for growth: "puppy", "junior" or "all life stages".

## Large breeds: the skeleton is sensitive

In large and giant breeds the skeleton grows for a long time, and fast. Two things can disturb it:

**1. Too much calcium.** Research at Utrecht University in Great Dane puppies showed that a long-term excess of calcium in the food led to abnormalities in the growth plates and cartilage, comparable to osteochondrosis (Goedegebuure & Hazewinkel, 1986). Young puppies are poorly protected against an excess: they absorb a lot of calcium even when there is too much in the food. That is why the FEDIAF guidelines give not only a minimum but also a **maximum** for calcium during growth; for the later growth phase it is 1.8 grams per 100 grams of dry matter.

**2. Growing too fast.** A puppy that gets too much energy grows faster than its skeleton can handle. Bigger puppies are not healthier puppies. A lean, steadily growing puppy is the goal: you can easily feel the ribs, and there is a visible waist.

What this means in practice:

- For large breeds, choose a puppy food explicitly made for them ("large breed" or "maxi").
- **Never add extra calcium or calcium supplements** to a complete puppy food. The food already contains enough.
- Feed measured portions and weigh your puppy regularly. Free feeding (a bowl that is always full) is not a good idea for large breeds.

## Small breeds: a lot of energy in a small stomach

Small breeds need a lot of energy per kilo of body weight and are fully grown quickly. A food with a somewhat higher energy density and small kibble suits them better. For very young, small puppies, spread the food over several meals a day.

## How long on puppy food?

A rough guide:

| Breed size (adult weight) | Puppy food until about |
| --- | --- |
| Small (up to 10 kg) | 10–12 months |
| Medium (10–25 kg) | 12 months |
| Large (25–45 kg) | 15–18 months |
| Giant (over 45 kg) | 18–24 months |

If in doubt, ask your vet, who can see whether growth has slowed down.
