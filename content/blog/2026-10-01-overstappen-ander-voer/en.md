You have found a food that scores better, suits your pet's age better, or leaves out the ingredient your pet cannot tolerate. Great! But don't put it in the bowl all at once tomorrow. A sudden change of food is one of the most common causes of loose stools, vomiting and wind.

## Why gradually?

The gut – and the bacteria that live in it – are tuned to what your pet eats now. A new food has a different make-up: other protein sources, more or less fat, different fibres. The digestive system needs a few days to adjust. Switch too fast and you will usually notice it in the litter box or on the pavement.

## The plan: 7 days

| Day | Old food | New food |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | 75% | 25% |
| 3–4 | 50% | 50% |
| 5–6 | 25% | 75% |
| 7 | – | 100% |

Does your pet have a sensitive stomach, or are you moving from dry to wet food (or the other way round)? Then take 10 to 14 days and stay a few days longer on each step.

## Mind the portion

A new food almost never contains exactly as many calories per 100 grams as the old one. Dry food is usually around 350–400 kcal per 100 grams, wet food often around 70–110. So weigh again. In your [pet's profile](/en/my-pet) Scanabowl works out, for every food, how many grams a day suit your pet's weight, age and activity.

## Cats are a different story

Cats can be very attached to what they know. A few tips:

- **Mix well**, so your cat cannot eat around the new food.
- **Warm wet food slightly.** Warm food smells stronger, and smell is decisive for cats.
- **Offer the new food in a small separate bowl first**, as an introduction.
- **Never let your cat "go hungry" until she eats the new food.** A cat that eats (almost) nothing for several days risks fatty liver disease (hepatic lipidosis) – a serious condition, especially in overweight cats. If your cat has not eaten for more than a day, contact your vet.

## What is normal – and what isn't?

Slightly softer stools or a bit more wind in the first days is normal. Go back one step in the plan and build up more slowly.

Contact your vet if you see:

- diarrhoea lasting more than a few days, or blood in the stools;
- repeated vomiting;
- lethargy or refusing to eat.

## When not to switch gradually

If your vet prescribes a veterinary diet, for example for bladder crystals or kidney disease, follow your vet's advice on how quickly to switch. The same applies if the current food is clearly causing problems.
