**Royal Canin** and **Hill's** are two names almost every pet owner recognizes — often recommended by vets, breeders, or specialist pet shops. Both brands offer huge ranges for dogs and cats, from puppy and kitten formulas to breed-specific kibble and veterinary diets.

But how do Royal Canin and Hill's compare when you look strictly and independently at the ingredient label and guaranteed analysis? We compared all **365 scored complete foods** from both brands in Scanabowl.

## Dog food: close on kibble, clear gap on wet food

For **dog kibble**, both brands sit very close together: **Royal Canin** averages **67/100** and **Hill's** averages **64/100**. Both rely heavily on grains such as corn, wheat, or rice for energy, and frequently use dehydrated poultry proteins or plant protein isolates rather than large shares of named fresh meat.

The real difference shows up in **wet dog food**:
- **Hill's wet dog food** averages **70/100**, more frequently listing named animal sources (such as chicken, turkey, or beef) clearly.
- **Royal Canin wet dog food** (especially pouches) averages **49/100**, mainly due to generic group declarations such as *"meat and animal derivatives"* without naming the species, alongside starch and sauce thickeners.

You can inspect every individual recipe on the brand pages for [Royal Canin](/en/foods/brand/royal-canin) and [Hill's](/en/foods/brand/hill-s).

## Cat food: Hill's scores noticeably higher

For **cat food**, the gap is wider. Cats are obligate carnivores that thrive on animal protein and fat with minimal carbohydrates.

- **Hill's cat food** averages **60/100** (dry 59, wet 60), with top recipes reaching 81/100.
- **Royal Canin cat food** averages **49/100** (dry 54, wet 43), held back by higher grain and plant-protein content in dry foods and unspecified animal derivatives in many wet pouches.

If you are looking for higher meat content and fewer fillers for a healthy cat, it is also worth comparing labels against brands like [Smølke](/en/foods/brand/smolke), [Edgard & Cooper](/en/foods/brand/edgard-en-cooper), or [Applaws](/en/foods/brand/applaws).

## Important context: what a label score does not measure

A fair comparison goes beyond a single number. Both Royal Canin and Hill's invest heavily in **nutritional research, strict quality control, and controlled feeding trials** — especially for clinical veterinary diets targeting kidney, urinary, or gastrointestinal conditions.

Scanabowl scores only what is publicly disclosed on the label: dry-matter nutrition, ingredient quality and transparency, and unnecessary additives. **If your dog or cat is on a prescription diet from Royal Canin or Hill's**, never switch away based on a label score alone — always consult your veterinarian first.

## Bottom line

- **Healthy dog (kibble):** On the label, Royal Canin (67) and Hill's (64) are closely matched. Always check the exact bag, as individual recipes range from 36 to 82.
- **Wet food or cat food:** **Hill's** clearly outperforms **Royal Canin** on label quality and transparency in our database.
