It is January. It is cold, wet and dark early. You may notice that your dog is a little less active than in summer: shorter walks in the rain, more naps by the radiator and sometimes changeable stools too.

In this post we look at the role of your dog’s gut and gut microbiome during the winter months.

## The connection between gut and wellbeing

Your dog’s gut does far more than simply digest food. The large intestine is home to the **gut microbiome**: an ecosystem of billions of bacteria that helps ferment fibres, build the immune system and produce short-chain fatty acids (such as butyrate) that nourish the gut wall.

Researchers also speak of the **gut-brain axis** in dogs: nerve pathways, hormones and signalling molecules connect the digestive tract with the nervous system. Disrupted gut flora (dysbiosis) or chronic digestive complaints in dogs frequently go hand in hand with changes in behaviour, restlessness or reduced appetite (**Pilla & Suchodolski, 2020**; **Mondo et al., 2020**).

## What does science say about fibres and prebiotics?

Veterinary literature (such as the review by **Pilla & Suchodolski, 2020** in *Veterinary Clinics: Small Animal Practice*) clearly shows that diet has the biggest influence on the composition of the canine gut microbiome:

- **Prebiotic fibres** (such as chicory inulin, fructo-oligosaccharides/FOS and beet pulp) are fermented by beneficial gut bacteria into short-chain fatty acids.
- Those short-chain fatty acids lower the pH in the gut, inhibit unwanted bacteria and support healthy stools.
- Sudden food changes or rich, one-sided holiday treats can upset that balance.

## What to look for on the ingredient list

Want to support your dog’s digestion and immunity? Look for these ingredients:

### ✅ Helpful supporters

- **Prebiotic fibre sources (chicory root, FOS, MOS, pumpkin or beet pulp):** feed beneficial bacteria in the large intestine and help keep stools firm.
- **Oily fish or salmon oil (source of EPA and DHA):** these long-chain omega-3 fatty acids support healthy skin, coat and mucous membranes (NRC, 2006).
- **Highly digestible animal proteins:** the better protein is digested in the small intestine, the fewer undigested protein residues reach the large intestine.

### ❌ Better avoided

- **Added sugars, caramel or syrups:** still regularly found in cheap dog treats and offer zero nutritional value for your dog.
- **Artificial colourings:** only there to please the owner’s eye and add nothing for the animal.
- **Sudden large portions of table scraps:** fatty or heavily seasoned food during the winter months is a common cause of acute diarrhoea in dogs.

## Practical tip for this week

Does your dog easily get soft stools or a rumbling tummy in winter?

1. **Keep the menu stable** and limit sugary treats.
2. **Consider adding a spoonful of pure cooked pumpkin purée** (100% pumpkin, with no spices or salt) or choosing a food with named prebiotics (such as chicory/FOS).
3. **Always build up fibre-rich additions gradually** over several days so the gut flora can adjust smoothly.
