A tin with nothing but tuna and a little broth. A pouch of chicken fillet in jelly. It looks pure and tasty – and your cat loves it. But take a close look at the back. Chances are it says **complementary feed**. And that means: this is not a complete meal.

## What the law says

In the European Union the types of animal feed are precisely defined (Regulation (EC) No 767/2009). For dog and cat food there are three you should know:

- **Complete feed** – food that, by reason of its composition, *is sufficient for a daily ration*. You can feed your pet this and nothing else.
- **Complementary feed** – food that is sufficient for a daily ration *only in combination with other feed*. Treats, chews, many "gourmet" wet foods with only meat or fish, and toppers fall into this group.
- **Dietetic feed** – food for a particular nutritional purpose, such as supporting kidney function or dissolving certain bladder stones. The label must state that purpose, and the advice is to consult a vet before use.

The manufacturer must state which type it is. You will usually find it just above or below the ingredient list.

## Why does it matter?

A dog or cat needs dozens of nutrients in the right proportions: not just protein and fat, but also calcium and phosphorus, vitamins, taurine (essential for cats) and trace elements. A complete food is formulated to contain all of them. Most European manufacturers use the nutritional guidelines of FEDIAF, the European pet food industry federation.

Pure fish or pure chicken does not have that balance. A tin of tuna, for example, contains plenty of phosphorus but hardly any calcium. As a treat or topper, complementary food is fine. As the only food in the long run, it can cause deficiencies.

That is why treats, toppers and other complementary foods show "Not scored". ## In short

- Does it say **complete feed**? Then it can be the whole meal.
- Does it say **complementary feed**? Give it as an extra next to a complete food, and count the calories.
- Does it say **dietetic feed**? Use it on your vet's advice.
