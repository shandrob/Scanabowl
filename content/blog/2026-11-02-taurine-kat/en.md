Look at the back of almost any bag of cat food and you will find it among the additives: **taurine**. It is there for a reason. For cats, taurine is an essential nutrient – and that was only discovered after things had gone badly wrong.

## What is taurine?

Taurine is a substance similar to an amino acid. It plays a role in the heart muscle, the retina, reproduction and the digestion of fat: cats use taurine to make bile. People and dogs can make most of their taurine themselves. **Cats make far too little** and also keep losing some through their bile. So they have to get it from their food.

## What happens with a deficiency

Two discoveries showed how important taurine is for cats:

- In **1975** researchers showed that cats on a diet with too little taurine developed a disease of the retina that can lead to **blindness** (Hayes, Carey & Schmidt, 1975).
- In **1987** researchers in California discovered that a common heart muscle disease in cats, **dilated cardiomyopathy**, was linked to low taurine levels in the blood. Cats given extra taurine recovered: their heart function returned to normal (Pion et al., 1987).

Manufacturers then raised the taurine content of cat food, and this form of heart disease became rare in cats. The European FEDIAF guidelines set a minimum taurine content for every complete cat food.

## Where is taurine found?

Taurine is found mainly in **animal tissue**: meat, fish and shellfish, and especially in heart and other organs. Plants contain virtually none. A cat that eats plenty of meat therefore gets taurine naturally. Commercial cat food usually has extra taurine added, because some is lost during processing.

## What does this mean in practice?

**Don't feed your cat dog food as her main meal.** Dog food is made for dogs, which can make taurine themselves. It often contains less taurine – and less of other nutrients cats need. A bite from the dog's bowl does no harm, but as a regular diet it is not suitable.

**Be careful with home-made food.** Taurine dissolves in water and is partly lost when cooking, especially if you throw away the cooking liquid. A home-made diet without proper calculation easily ends up with too little. Have a recipe made by a vet specialised in nutrition.

**Vegetarian or vegan cat food** is only possible if taurine – and other nutrients cats cannot get from plants, such as ready-made vitamin A – has been added, and if the food meets the standards for a complete food. Talk to your vet before considering it, and never make it yourself.

## What about dogs?

Dogs usually make taurine themselves from other amino acids. Even so, low taurine levels have been found in some dogs with heart muscle disease, including in the research on grain-free food. Read more in [is grain-free food better for your dog or cat?](/en/blog/2026-10-12-graanvrij-voer-beter).
