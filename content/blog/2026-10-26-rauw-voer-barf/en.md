More and more dogs and cats eat raw meat: ready-made from the freezer, or put together at home. Supporters see a shiny coat, better stools and a more natural diet. Critics point to bacteria and deficiencies. What do we actually know?

## The benefits: mainly experience, little research

Many benefits owners mention – a nicer coat, less tartar, smaller stools, more energy – are familiar, but largely based on experience. Well-designed studies comparing raw food with a complete cooked food are scarce. That does not mean the benefits don't exist, only that they have not been well demonstrated.

## The risks: bacteria and parasites

This has been researched, including in the Netherlands. Researchers at Utrecht University bought **35 frozen products** from eight raw food brands and tested them in the lab (van Bree et al., 2018). They found:

- **Listeria monocytogenes** in 54% of the products;
- **Salmonella** in 20%;
- **E. coli O157:H7**, a bacterium that can cause serious illness in people, in 23%;
- E. coli resistant to important antibiotics (ESBL) in **80%**;
- parasites such as *Sarcocystis* and *Toxoplasma* in a smaller share of the products.

Freezing does not reliably kill bacteria. Your pet can get ill itself, but it can also shed the bacteria without showing any signs – and infect people in the home. A large British review came to the same conclusion about the microbiological risks (Davies et al., 2019).

## The second risk: an incomplete diet

A ready-made raw food sold as a **complete feed** has to meet the same nutritional standards as any other complete food. With home-made diets that is harder: without calculation, the balance of calcium and phosphorus, vitamins and trace elements is easily off. For growing puppies and kittens this can have serious consequences. If you want to make your own, have a recipe made by a vet specialised in nutrition.

## Feeding raw? Do it as safely as possible

- **Thaw in the fridge**, covered and on the bottom shelf, not on the counter.
- Use a **separate chopping board and separate bowls**, and wash them hot after every use.
- **Wash your hands** after feeding and after picking up stools.
- Don't leave raw food in the bowl for hours; remove leftovers after about half an hour.
- Be extra careful – or choose a cooked food – if **young children, older people, pregnant women or people with a weakened immune system** live in the home.
- Pick up stools in the garden straight away.

## How Scanabowl rates raw food

Ready-made raw food is listed in our database as **frozen food**. If it is a complete food, it gets a score with exactly the same method as kibble and wet food: based on ingredients and nutrient values. Important to know: **that score says nothing about the microbiological safety** of a product. You cannot see that on a label.
