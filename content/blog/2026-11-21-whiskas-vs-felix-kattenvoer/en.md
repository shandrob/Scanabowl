## The biggest takeaway: dry kibble vs. wet pouches

One pattern jumps off the page: **for Whiskas and Felix, dry kibble is by far the weakest part of the range** (averaging **31–34/100**), while wet pouches score noticeably better (around **50/100**).

Why do budget supermarket kibbles score so low?
1. **Heavy on grains and carbs:** Cereals often sit at #1 on the ingredient list, even though cats are obligate carnivores designed for animal protein and fat.
2. **Vague animal declarations:** Group labels like *"meat and animal derivatives (4% chicken)"* leave most of the animal species undisclosed.
3. **Added sugars or colorants:** More common in budget formulas to improve appearance or palatability.

## Is Whiskas or Felix "unsafe"?

No. Every complete cat food sold in Europe must meet legal FEDIAF baseline standards for essential nutrients like taurine, vitamins, and minerals. It will not cause acute deficiencies.

However, with cheap dry kibble you are paying mainly for carbohydrates and unspecified derivatives — and dry-only diets can also contribute to low water intake. Read more in [Why wet food for cats is more than a treat](/en/blog/2026-09-28-natvoer-kat-water).

## Practical upgrades without breaking the bank

- **Quickest win:** If your cat currently eats Whiskas or Felix kibble, moving to a moderately priced brand with named meat — like **[Purina One](/en/foods/brand/purina-one)** (dry avg 61), **[Smølke](/en/foods/brand/smolke)** (avg 74), or **[Edgard & Cooper](/en/foods/brand/edgard-en-cooper)** (avg 76) — makes an immediate jump in label quality.
- **Top-scoring options:** Brands such as **[Almo Nature](/en/foods/brand/almo-nature)** (avg 82), **[Applaws](/en/foods/brand/applaws)** (avg 85), **[Acana](/en/foods/brand/acana)** (avg 87), and **[Orijen](/en/foods/brand/orijen)** (avg 93) sit near the top of our database. Just always check whether a tin is labeled **complete** vs. **complementary**.
