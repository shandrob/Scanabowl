Standing in front of the shelf of bags of kibble and tins of pâté, wondering which is better for your dog or cat? Both types of food can be complete and nutritious, but they differ considerably in moisture content, convenience and price. Here we clearly lay out the pros and cons of dry food and wet food.

## Dry food: practical and concentrated

Kibble is the default choice for many owners. Dry food contains very little water — usually around 10 per cent moisture — meaning the nutrients are highly concentrated in every gram. That generally makes a bag of kibble better value per portion than wet food.

Dry food is also easy to use. You can store it for a long time, it smells less strongly and you can weigh out a portion quickly. For animals that eat small amounts throughout the day, it stays fresh in the bowl for longer. Do make sure to seal opened bags properly so the fats do not go rancid.

However, dry food requires extra attention to drinking. Because there is barely any moisture in the kibble, your pet has to get all the water it needs from its water bowl. Cats in particular often drink little by nature.

## Wet food: extra moisture and often more appealing

Wet food — such as tins, trays or pouches — consists largely of water, often 70 to 85 per cent. Your dog or cat therefore takes in plenty of moisture straight away while eating. For animals that drink little, and certainly for cats, that is an easy way to increase total fluid intake.

Many pets also find wet food tastier. Thanks to its strong smell and soft texture, fussy eaters or older animals often finish it more easily. Because wet food contains fewer calories per 100 grams than dry food, a portion provides a relatively large volume in the stomach.

The downside is mainly practical. Wet food is usually more expensive per day, takes up more cupboard space and spoils faster once opened. Keep an opened tin covered in the fridge and ideally use it up within one to two days.

## Myths and combining: what works in practice?

There are a few persistent assumptions surrounding kibble and wet food. For instance, many people think that kibble automatically keeps teeth clean. In practice, many dogs and cats swallow ordinary kibble quickly, or it breaks right on the first bite. It therefore does not replace toothbrushing; regular brushing remains the most reliable foundation for dental care.

Nor is one type of food inherently healthier than the other. Both dry food and wet food can be a **complete pet food** that meets the nutritional guidelines of **FEDIAF** (the European pet food federation). The real difference lies in the quality of the ingredients and the ratio of proteins, fats, vitamins and minerals.

You do not have to choose, by the way. **Combining** — for example, kibble and a portion of wet food a day — gives you both the convenience of dry food and the extra moisture of wet food. Just be sure to balance the portions against each other so your pet does not take in too much energy overall.

## Checklist for your choice

Every dog and cat is different. Use these points to work out what suits your pet:

- **Check that it is complete:** choose **complete pet food** as a main meal, not complementary food.
- **Look at drinking habits:** if your cat or dog drinks little on its own, wet food or combining is often a good choice.
- **Consider teeth and appetite:** older animals or fussy eaters sometimes benefit from softer wet food.
- **Calculate the amount:** if you combine kibble and wet food, adjust both portions according to the feeding tables.
- **Store food properly:** keep kibble dry and sealed, and keep opened wet food chilled.
